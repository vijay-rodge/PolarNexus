DOCUMENT TITLE: India's Polar Journey: Educational Outreach Guide for Students & Schools
SOURCE: National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research Outreach Division
AUDIENCE: School Students, Educators, and the General Public

SECTION 1: WHY DOES INDIA STUDY THE POLAR REGIONS?
The polar regions—Antarctica at the South Pole and the Arctic at the North Pole—along with the Himalayan glaciers (often called the 'Third Pole') are Earth's giant refrigerators!
Even though India is thousands of kilometers away, polar ice regulates global sea levels, ocean currents, and directly influences the Indian Monsoon. When polar winds shift or Arctic sea ice melts, rainfall patterns over India change.

SECTION 2: MEET INDIA'S POLAR STATIONS
1. Maitri (Antarctica): Opened in 1989, built on solid rock in the Schirmacher Oasis next to the beautiful freshwater Lake Priyadarshini.
2. Bharati (Antarctica): Opened in 2012, an ultra-modern eco-friendly station built from shipping containers on stilts with its own satellite dish to communicate with space.
3. Himadri (Arctic): Opened in 2008 in the village of Ny-Ålesund in Norway's Svalbard archipelago, where reindeer and polar bears roam.
4. IndARC (Arctic): A magical underwater laboratory anchored deep in an Arctic fjord, listening to whales and measuring ice-cold ocean currents all year round.
5. Himansh (Himalayas): Situated high in Spits valley to study glacier melting.

SECTION 3: DAILY LIFE OF A POLAR SCIENTIST
In Antarctica, winter lasts months without the sun ever rising! Scientists wear four layers of thermal clothing, eat nutritious freeze-dried and greenhouse-grown food, and brave temperatures colder than -40°C to study auroras, penguins, and cosmic rays.
