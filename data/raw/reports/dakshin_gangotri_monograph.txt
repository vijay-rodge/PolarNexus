DOCUMENT TITLE: Dakshin Gangotri - Historical Monograph of India's First Antarctic Base
SOURCE: National Centre for Polar and Ocean Research Historical Archives
STATION: Dakshin Gangotri
REGION: Princess Astrid Coast, Queen Maud Land, Antarctica (70°05' S, 12°00' E)
COMMISSIONED: 1983-1984
DECOMMISSIONED: 1990

SECTION 1: HISTORICAL FOUNDATION
Dakshin Gangotri was established during the 3rd Indian Scientific Expedition to Antarctica (1983-1984), led by Dr. H.K. Gupta. It was erected on the ice shelf of Princess Astrid Coast in a record time of eight weeks by Indian Army engineers and scientists. It marked India's entry as a Consultative Party to the Antarctic Treaty.

SECTION 2: OPERATIONAL LIFE AND DECOMMISSIONING
Dakshin Gangotri functioned as a winter base from 1984 to 1989. Over five years, excessive accumulation of drifting snow buried the structure under more than 10 meters of ice. In 1990, it was decommissioned and designated as a historic site. Maitri station was established on rocky bedrock as its permanent successor.
